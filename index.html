About

After completing a PhD at the Biological Station of Roscoff with Myriam Valero (CNRS) and Denis Roze (CNRS), I am currently a postdoc in Nick Barton's group at ISTA (Institute of Science and Technology Austria).

My recent work focuses on adaptation in spatially structured populations — modelled using either stepping-stone or island schemes — looking in particular at two angles: the effect of reproductive systems (mainly clonality), and the effect of genetic architecture on adaptive trajectories.

02

Research

Reproductive systems & adaptation

How clonality changes the way a structured population adapts to a heterogeneous environment or face recurrent deleterious mutations.

Genetic architecture

The role of the number, effect size, and distribution of loci involved in adaptation of spatially structured populations.

Range limits

Does the emergence of range limits depend on the genetic architecture? What happens once clonal reproduction is favoured in range limits (geographic parthenogenesis, Fouqueau & Roze 2021), can the population ever expand?

03

Projects

Clonal reproduction in an environmental gradient

With Nathanaël Boutillon (Uni Wien), we have explored how many different types of clones can coexist along an environmental gradient, by considering one quantitative trait under stabilizing selection.

View project

When do we expect a trait with a finite number of loci to be well described by the infinitesimal model?

With Martin Gbúr (ISTA), we explore why and over what timescales the infinitesimal model accurately describes the inheritance and evolution of quantitative traits. In particular, how does its accuracy depend on the finite genetic architecture underlying the trait and how is it affected by selection, drift, and mutation.

Clines in quantitative traits

When do we expect clines to form? What can it tell about genomic offsets? Future projects with Nicolas Bierne (ISEM), Himani Sachdeva (Uni Wien), John Welsh (Cambridge)... More description to come!
